# Chapter 1: The Illusion of Growth
## Section 1: The beginning of the story

<!-- Guide: Introduction to: Shows the false prosperity of the tower and how Lyra obtains the package. Establishes the context and tone. Hooks the reader with broken daily life or the beginning of action. -->

Tower 47 kept going up in price.

Every week, an email-like notification celebrated the increase on the bright, warm, and attractive screen projected just feet from its inhabitants' retinas. A certain percentage more. A new record. Neighbors commented on it in the elevators with a mix of relief and superiority, their lives justified by the price of concrete and glass, their decisions becoming correct, their intelligence greater, and their self-esteem lifting them higher and higher above the rest of the city. Every day a news piece confirmed the immovability of their successes, every hour the intense green color of the chart became an even more certain, more solid, more real confirmation. The light of dawn pierced the neon screens with its intense activity, its vortex of colors, information, and numbers drilling into the pupils of its spectators like the sun penetrating the starry sky - blue now by its presence - at the beginning of the days. Its glare heralded infinite floods and a future of prosperity based on patrimonial present.

Lyra crossed the lobby without looking at the screens. She knew what they would say before they turned on. "Sustained growth." "Structural stability." "Market confidence." Each news story heralded the triumph of a new resilience plan, a new adaptation strategy to the new environment, a record budget with which to carry out the new "Leap Forward" promised forever and ever.

Outside, the air was denser than the previous month. The reports didn't say so, but you could tell when you breathed.

She activated the panel on her wrist. Her balance had gone down again. Not from a specific expense, but from all of them at once: energy, network access, building maintenance. Everything went up. Except for her.

In the elevator, a man smiled while checking the updated appraisal of his apartment. It was higher than the previous month. His social class took off, his patrimony pointed to the sky, the envy of those who didn't own apartments dissipated with each new increase and his borrowing capacity was extremely high. He could start thinking about carrying out new plans formerly forbidden to him. He could move to a larger apartment, to a house with a garden or to a more exclusive area, better. After all, he deserved it. He had escaped the trap of living without property. He took a risk at the right time and was now reaping the fruits of his audacity, his intelligence and his effort. Banks granted him credits with astonishing ease and his future options grew in geometric proportion. The rest, angry, defeated and frustrated could only complain and blame others. But for that man, the reality was that all those miserable people were miserable as a result of their own decisions. From their desire to travel and see the world to their concern for food.

"Another three percent," he said, without looking at anyone in particular. "This cannot fall."

Lyra watched him for a second.

It wasn't a statement. It was a plea.

When the doors opened on the ground floor, the city welcomed her with its usual noise: delivery drones, personalized ads, civic alerts.

Everything worked. It seemed perfect. It was the future.

Everything was getting worse.

And nobody seemed to notice the difference.

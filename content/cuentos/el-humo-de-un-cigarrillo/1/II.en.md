# II

"She was white, haggard, and broken like a snow-capped peak. The empty cleft of her eyes glowed yellowish and discordant, and her faded dress levitated pale and withered like a rose. She was the living image of Time, but an atrocious time, a harmful, mendacious, abject time; a murderer. She was the Past remembering its existence, and in the background her singing voice flooded the room, sweet as the voice of a Spanish woman and sugared like a Dominican accent; it was thick, thick and dense, and poured golden like honey and amber over the auditory sense. And she laid her hand upon me. Yes, her glacial touch made contact with my warm flesh. And she was beautiful. Her hand was beautiful, like her voice, fine and graceful, feminine and coquettish; a lover. She was like the fog that settles over a frozen lake in the marshes and along the marine horizon, and her phalanges foamed like crystalline rivers embroidered with the brilliance of a diamond and the freshness of sea foam. Oh! She was beautiful. Yes! She was exceedingly beautiful! She possessed the fine charm of platinum and the elegant snowy tone of the storm, and she smiled, she smiled swollen with happiness. An innocent happiness, the joy of a cruel and ruthless little girl. Then she touched me, and I was able to behold within her touch. Yes! I swear it! I could see within her touch and I saw, I saw the past."

"I saw Her stalking young Humanity, rehearsing her lethal designs, accumulating horrifying knowledge and nuances of macabre wisdom that make the heart tremble. Twelve thousand years or more have passed since she tested her first work upon us, and her final lashes are still being felt. Yes, she showed me the faces of millions of disfigured people, their complexions withered by pustules like the face of Ferdinand II de' Medici, and I saw the pain of families, of wives, of fathers, of children. I contemplated the color of the altered sky and the sea shadowed by the scream of a mother in pain, much like that painting by Munch. There were centuries upon centuries of a desolation from which no one escaped—not the young, nor the adult, nor the newborn—and under which Horus himself, Almighty, King of Upper and Lower Egypt, Son of Ra, cast in the burning gold of the desert, Ramses V, succumbed."

"But she was not satisfied and soon created a new pandemic. From the highest tower of her misty palace, studded with ivory and frigid ice and wandering, like the cloud upon which it sits, she sprinkled droplets of vapor inflamed with measles and then simply dedicated herself to contemplating with immense pleasure the anguish of the fallen while the light of the lightning bolts illuminated her sinister smile."

"And not content with the suffering she had caused, on the darkest night that has ever existed, she spawned the blackest creature that could ever be known; small fragments of her dissolute soul that devoured, under the horrifying name of the Black Death, entire cities whose inhabitants decayed into horrendous blisters. And inside her, I only found cruelty, just as in the faces of the murderers of Caesar by Camuccini, from whose eyes Hatred spills forth."

"But never, ever, had she enjoyed herself so much as when she sickened the inert brains of the entire Planet so that they would embark on two immense wars, while devouring their lungs through the Spanish Flu. I could feel her terrifying laughter puffing in my ears and piercing my heart like an icy dagger. She took with her, in one fell swoop, millions of people. However, her final move is even more wicked, even more than calling her daughter Famine, for, by disguising herself as Pleasure, she needs only to sit and wait to be called, implored one might say. And I am cursed, I know it, she confessed it to me."

"Pleasure? What are you insinuating? Now I am certain you are laughing at me. Please, stop, it is not the least bit funny. I implore you."

"I speak of the pleasure of a cigarette, Amaranto. It fascinates us. And not only does it fascinate us, but it also provokes a sensation of overwhelming superiority that enchants us. The mere act of lighting the smoking torch of vehemence and swallowing the smoke, feeling how Death penetrates our interior and caresses our throat and our lungs before being expelled from our body at our command with a mere and charming sigh, is delicious to us. We feel powerful when, with a slight gesture of our fingertips, we extinguish it, and we believe ourselves safe because no one, save ourselves, decides when to kiss her scarlet lips. But it is not true, we are never safe from Her. She is patient, and that is her greatest success: it is we who hand over our soul to her, and we do so slowly and pleasantly by our own free will. Yes, my dear friend, She showed it to me with a gleam of intelligence deep within her vacant gaze: Death is the smoke of a cigarette. But let us forget the matter: the wandering finger of dawn caresses our eyelids and the fresh morning breeze murmurs poetry. A bird sings and we live, Amaranto, we live! Let us go outside to catch some air, let the sun's rays warm our faces and the green leaves of the trees cover our thoughts."

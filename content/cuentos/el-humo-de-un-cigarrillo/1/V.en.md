# V

A certain feline strolled along the sill of an open window. Dark as the night, the light from inside reflected in its feline irises. Inside the Summer House, a few nervous, altered footsteps paced around a black telephone. It was Lirio who, unable to deduce who that strange man was that had announced his death and, already lacking the necessary will to push him out of his thoughts, was trying to find a solution to his uncertainty. When would it happen? If only he had a specific date, place, or time, he could—simply—prepare himself. It would be possible for him to brace his mind and even decide, with a certain elegance, how to bid farewell to his own life. On the contrary, he had no choice but to live with the same ignorance regarding the matter as the rest of mortals, except that for him, the hope of a still-long life, another year left to go, or the promise of an endless month, were not possible. He had only one way out: to discover who would execute the plan that Death—with her impeccable and snowy hands—had written long ago in neat and indelible ink upon the crystalline book of Destiny.

He held the phone in his hands. He thought for a moment while picturing himself calmer than he actually was. And he dialed the number of the only person whose voice pleased him more than any broken silence.

"Yes?"

"Hello, Alina."

"You have finally deigned to call me. I've been dying of boredom for years in front of the phone."

"Do you know anything about that man I mentioned to you? I need to find him. To know who he is. What he does. How he is going to do it."

"How he is going to do what? Lirio, you are acting a bit strange. You speak the same as always, but you sound very nervous. As if something were hurting you too much."

"Listen to me, Alina. I beg of you. It is a matter of life and death. I need to speak with that man. Perhaps it is still not too late and everything can be avoided. Maybe it was just a warning. Yes, that's it. It is a warning. I am still too young. That is why they have decided to make me understand that I must make better use of my life. Enough of wandering from one place to another. Of facing life like a mere dilettante. Life is very serious. There are no more opportunities. They are a fallacy. I must work on my music. I have to finish all those symphonies that have been hovering in my head since forever. That's it! How blind I have been! It was nothing more than a sign. It is impossible for anything to happen to me. But that man, that man seemed to speak so seriously in his note that I cannot stop thinking about it."

"Lirio, please, calm down. Who would want to do something to you and why? And above all, what is he going to do to you? I have already told you. That man you speak of does not even seem to have left a trace. It is as if he had never been at that dinner. Are you sure you really saw him? You know that the champagne and my neckline can be very intoxicating."

"I am not crazy! For God's sake! Alina! I swear to you that I saw him. He didn't stop looking at me throughout the entire dinner. And the note. The note he wrote. That note is real. I still have it. It threatens me with death."

"Lirio, the waiter you described assures me that he did not deliver any note."

"He lies. I am sure he lies. The coward doesn't want to bear any responsibility in the matter. But it is true. It is real. Death is pursuing me."

"Calm down, please. I am going to hang up now. But do not continue to exacerbate your nerves. Give me some time. I will stop by your house as soon as I can. Please, please Lirio, take good care of yourself. Promise me."

"Alright. Yes, I know what I will do. I will work on my music. I will write a nocturne with your name. You'll see. It will be beautiful. See you soon."

"See you soon."

After hanging up the phone, Lirio sat down at the piano. He took one of his blonde cigarettes and lit it. The ember sparkled with the fire of Hell. He took a drag and released the smoke relaxedly, as if he meant to convince himself that he felt calm by doing everything more slowly. The symphony traveled through the nerves of his hand, guided partly by an awake and happy corner of his mind, and partly by a tired, sad, and brooding heart, until it reached the keys of the piano.

It was a nocturne. A marvelous nocturne. Each note seemed to converse with the previous one, lingering in the air for a few seconds like a living and magical entity, whose life was carried out in an instant. With what delicacy they vibrated in the air, almost like an imaginary flower opening and closing. Its notes arranged themselves like a wonderful lake hidden in the middle of the forest, and the constancy of the sound established a delicious tide that never ceased to come and go, reflecting blue and white flashes upon its surface—precious moonlight originating from an infinite and expansive tale that marched ceaselessly, and inexorably, towards its end, always within the human being who contained it and who was capable of bringing it to light, alone, through the keys of a piano.

Days later, in that very same house, they found Lirio with cold hands upon the piano. He bore the face of a peaceful being, and the dark feline that lately slumbered beneath the serene music and upon the sill of the windows was never seen there again.

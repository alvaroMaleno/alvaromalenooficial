# IV

The opera house filled an increasingly dark night with light. Nothing around it remained unlit, and the warmth of its interior emanated firm and strong toward the outside. It was like a drop of gold set in the immense jet-black heart of the city. The colonnade—elegant—seemed to support a magnificent frieze like an imposing muscle, and upon the frieze stood Art, riding four steeds as swift as fire and burning like the densest rage. It was a spectacle in itself, and like a Russian doll, it would harbor a copy of itself within, at least until, like the immense roar of a lion, it folded Ignorance and cast its light toward a world that still yearned to shine.

It was concert night. It was a night of emotion. It was a night in all its splendor. The ladies strolled about, unruly as the breeze, and the gentlemen, restless, stood upon their leather shoes without modifying the mold of their dark suits in the slightest. No trouble is too much when presenting oneself before Art.

Lirio was already turning the corner with a hurried air, planting himself before the light that shone brightest for him in that moment. His face seemed to hold a certain internal relief, perhaps originating from the outside.

"Is someone chasing you?"

"Time, Alina. It is always the same. It constantly drives me to despair, reminding me of how short this life is and how long the days I spend separated from you." It was Lirio who answered.

"Oh! You do not spend all your time with me simply because you don't want to. You are always slipping away from me like a defenseless little animal. Everyone has been talking about it for some time."

"And what exactly are they saying?"

"They say: 'Lirio? We really mistook him for a huge and fierce wolf. And yet, look at how he does nothing but hide when Mrs. de Casas appears. Is he afraid of such a pleasant being?' And many other worse things that I will not dare repeat out of the affection I hold for you."

"It is always good to know that one's misfortune serves as praise for one's closest friends."

"Oh! Once again. Do you have such a sharp tongue for speaking the language of music as well?"

The crowd was already walking towards the interior, illuminated to the very last corner. It was not cluttered. It was warm and seeped like a spring into the most open minds within. Not a single space remained that had not been filled with beauty.

"Thousands of lines also constitute something beautiful. Don't you think so, Lirio?"

"Without a doubt. Simplicity is beautiful, but Beauty is only simple to the one who is already simple."

"And the more you know how to appreciate it, the more forms it takes."

"Like your intelligence, dazzling tonight."

"Oh! You surprise me again. Hush, let us sit down, let us listen to that demigoddess who brings us music from the beyond, and let us enjoy, Lirio, let us enjoy as if it were the only night we could do so. Let us enjoy as if we knew how to do it in the best possible way."

"I am all ears, and heart."

But a pang pierced Lirio's heart as he uttered his final words. Alina's voice whispered ceaselessly behind his ear: "as if it were the only night we could do so." And perhaps, deep down, he could not understand it any other way. A less painful way for him. One that did not cause him as much terror as that one. The letter he had received from a stranger appeared time and again in his imagination as an inescapable prophecy. The man from the party was still watching him from afar. He showed no mercy, no compassion. The cold hand of death seemed to have placed its fingers on one of his shoulders, never to let go. The Adrianople curtain opened to reveal the singer entirely covered by shadows, only her illuminated lips distinguishable at the exact moment a faint note crossed the doors from an unknown dimension into ours, as if it were an advance party on a reconnaissance mission, while Lirio felt his own blood spilling from his head, completely covering his body.

Only during the intermission between acts was Lirio able to utter a word. He did so to ask—somewhat nervously—Mrs. de Casas about the man whose presence had disturbed him so much during the dinner where they had last seen each other.

"He does not ring a bell at all." That was the answer he obtained.

"I am certainly tremendously interested in him. He even went so far as to write me a note. He was seated several tables away, behind ours. He occupied the same spot as Mr. Ramos at our table. He was wearing a tuxedo and had very thick eyebrows. Other than that, he did not draw the slightest bit of attention."

"Still rings no bells. However, I will ask the hosts. Also the maître d', whom I will see very soon, since I am thinking of organizing an intimate dinner at home, to see if he knows which waiter was entrusted with handing you that letter. Perhaps he knows something more."

"Would you mind sending me a message as soon as you know something?"

"Of course. I will write you an email myself."

The spectacle ended. Everyone left separately to finish the day in their own way. Barely a week later, Lirio received the much-anticipated email:

"No one knows of such a curious guest.

Yours always, A. de Casas."

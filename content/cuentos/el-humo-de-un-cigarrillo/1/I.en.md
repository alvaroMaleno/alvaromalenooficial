# I

Between the immense windows of the Summer House and the dazzling curtains of satin blue, upon which a delicious Chopin nocturne settled and the fresh breeze swayed, the Night showed its swarthy calm, studded with carmine and mauve flashes and white stars. Indeed, inside the only house illuminated in the early hours of the morning, someone was playing the piano marvelously well, and alongside the pulsating beams of light from the window frames, a dark quietude and a gloomy melancholy slipped through, to whose perfume gallant nocturnal butterflies flocked, along with silences prolonged by the rhythm of the full Moon and the pearly dress of the roses.

Suddenly, the piano interrupted its sharp discourse, and the black kitty—jet-black like nothingness and somber as the future, whose amber irises gleamed like the saffron song of the Afternoon—who had come to slumber at the edge of the bluish parterre and beneath the powdery whisper of a fairy, could perfectly hear Lirio murmur to his interlocutor, after exchanging the uncomfortable piano bench for the velvety yellow of the plush armchair, in front of which stood a lacquered oak table and the attentive gaze of Amaranto:

"Oh! Please, Amaranto, hand me a cigarette, I find myself exceedingly weary of melancholy this beautiful night. Although it is true that there is no sentiment quite as noble in our spectrum of colors—for not only does it possess the powerful force that drives us to solitude, the mother of all true work and faithful lover of pleasure, but also, by its very nature, it can be the mysterious scourge that expels us from it, reminding us that we were not born to live alone, taking pleasure in dragging us back to the boring and sterile society of men—it is equally true that the delight of conversation in extraordinary moments surpasses the delicious pleasure of speaking with oneself."

"Here you go, my dear Lirio. You shouldn't abuse cigarettes, at least not as much and as often as you do; some think that Death hides within them."

A few seconds passed in silence.

"Oh! And it is not a mere belief at all. On the contrary, it is a Truth, an irrefutable Truth, Amaranto, my friend. I have proven it myself. But do me a favor, hand me another one, I still feel unsatisfied, and it is absurd for me to worry about my life, for my destiny has already been written."

"What do you mean, Lirio? Have you died? Are you going to die? Has something happened that I am unaware of? Tell me, I beg you, I hold my heart in the palm of my hand, and only you, only you, can stop it from leaping into the void forever. But tell me, please, are you alright? So that is why I find you so melancholic tonight."

"I have seen Her, Amaranto. I have seen The Lady in White and She has touched me, She has rested Her icy fingers upon my contracted skin. I know it, it was a warning, a vague, indefinite warning, but definitive after all, and I know She will soon return for me. I am already cursed, and to be cursed is to be almost dead. But no, do not weep for me, my dear brother, soon nothing will matter and nothing will remain of me but the perfume of a flower—ephemeral, yes, but immense as the wind itself and delicious as the sea mist."

"Come now, Lirio! Don't joke with me! Don't frighten me like this. Even if you had seen Her, it could be nothing more than your imagination. Forget it, please, and play, play another song! Only music has the quality of being everything while being nothing, without possessing a clear logical structure; perhaps that is why we fill it with meaning, pouring our souls into it just as the writer or the poet spills theirs onto the paper. Without a doubt, it inspired every metamorphosis ever created, and without any doubt, it is the complex reflection of our reality, cold and empty until we fulfill it with our thoughts. Please, play, and let us weep; let us weep until we have no tears left and only smiles can bloom."

"I am not lying; I detest lies and you know it. I have only lied in my life out of fear or for failing to find another reasonable way out. In fact, I consider the Lie to be the cause of Politics, and there is nothing more vulgar or clumsy than Politics. It is always afraid of being discovered. That is why it lies. What I have told you is true, I know it, and She is absolutely not as everyone thinks. It happened just yesterday, in this very room. I was playing the piano and smoking a mild cigarette—blonde as straw, sandy to the taste and sensitive to thought like a good mood—and, after exhaling a deep drag very slowly, right behind the smoke of the cigarette, She appeared to me."

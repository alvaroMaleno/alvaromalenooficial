Minimalism is not just about having fewer material things, but about making room for what truly matters. In this first article, we will explore the roots of this philosophy and how to apply it in our daily lives.

## The First Step

To begin, we must observe our surroundings and ask ourselves what objects bring us real value. Anything that does not serve a purpose or bring us joy is likely occupying space that could be used for mental peace.

Did we choose them, or did those objects choose us?
It is not about renouncing the material world, but about truly choosing what adds value to us and eliminating what subtracts from it.

Guilt is not a good advisor on this path. It is preferable to act with intention and deliberation rather than with remorse.

## What brings us value?

Did we make that last purchase because we were truly looking for something similar, or because the mere act of buying produced a feeling of novelty and ephemeral satisfaction?

Is our life so hard that we need instant gratification? Or are we just bored? What do we expect from that object that we are not capable of giving to ourselves?

These and other questions are useful when evaluating and pausing at the moment of purchase. However, once we master our impulses, how do we decide what brings us value?

It is simple: does it drive us to action, or to passivity? Is the action good, satisfying, or does it induce us to improve? If the answer is yes, then it is an object that brings us value.

And if it drives us to passivity? It is probably not an object that brings us value.
We could venture to say that it even subtracts value from us. Human value, because it empties us of the action that makes us who we are. And social value, because it turns us into a mere receptacle for that which serves a purpose foreign to us as a whole.
